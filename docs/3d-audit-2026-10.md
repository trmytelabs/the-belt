# 3D models and animation audit (October 2026)

An audit of the 3D depot's models, animation, vehicles, lighting and cameras as
they stand on `feat/3d-realism-pass` (commit `dee6793`), written before any
realism strategy is chosen. It changes no game code. The legacy 2D depot and
classic game are out of scope.

Everything rated here was viewed in the baseline screenshots under
[`docs/images/audit-2026-10/`](images/audit-2026-10/), captured by
[`tools/audit/capture-3d-shots.mjs`](../tools/audit/capture-3d-shots.mjs) at
fixed, recorded camera positions so the same shots can be retaken after a
change. Code references give the file and line on this branch.

### Merged and instanced meshes

`merge.ts` (`mergeStatic`, built on `BufferGeometryUtils.mergeGeometries`)
groups meshes by material, bakes colours into vertex colours and skips skinned
meshes. It is applied to the depot scene and every truck (`world.ts:219–230`),
every character's procedural figure (`characters.ts:535`), the story stages
(`story.ts:330`), the mall, the driving scene, the Kenney models, the parcels
and **the imported sedans** (`propModels.ts:50`), which is why their wheels
cannot turn. `InstancedMesh` is used for the floor piles (`world.ts:1067`) and
wall piles (`wallPiles.ts:203`). There is no `THREE.LOD` anywhere.

### Textures

Five CC0 PBR sets in `public/textures/` (concrete, rubber, steel, paint,
cardboard) as colour + OpenGL normal + packed ARM WebP files at 512 or 1024 px,
about 850 KB in total, wired through `surfaceMaterial()` (`textures.ts:89`).
Signs, road markings, TV screens, chain-link and the mall use canvas-generated
textures. The imported models carry their own embedded textures.

### Renderer, lighting and materials

`WebGLRenderer({ antialias, powerPreference: 'high-performance' })`, ACES
filmic tone mapping at exposure 1.18, PCF soft shadows on GPU renderers, pixel
ratio capped at 2 (`main3d.ts:189–235`). On GPU renderers the frame goes
through a multisampled half-float `EffectComposer`: `RenderPass`, a
half-resolution GTAO pass that reuses the scene depth (`ambientOcclusion.ts`),
`UnrealBloomPass(0.55, 0.5, 0.96)` and `OutputPass`. Only the depot scene has
an environment map (`RoomEnvironment` through `PMREMGenerator`, intensity
0.18); the story, driving and dream scenes have none, so nothing in them can
reflect anything. Lights are a hemisphere plus one shadowed directional per
scene and point lights (up to 11 sodium lamps in the depot). Almost every
material is `MeshStandardMaterial`; the fish tank is the only
`MeshPhysicalMaterial`, and there are no Lambert, Phong or custom shader
materials. The truck cutaway patches materials through `onBeforeCompile`.

## 2. Baseline screenshots

Captured on this branch with headless Microsoft Edge on an NVIDIA RTX 4090
(ANGLE Direct3D 11), 1280×800 at device pixel ratio 1, `quality === 'high'`,
with the production composer (GTAO and bloom) exactly as in play, HUD included.

To retake every shot after a change:

```bash
npm run build
BELT_BROWSER_EXECUTABLE="C:/Program Files (x86)/Microsoft/Edge/Application/msedge.exe" BELT_3D_GPU=1 node tools/audit/capture-3d-shots.mjs
```

The script stops the render loop, replaces `performance.now()` with a clock it
advances itself and seeds `Math.random`, so the story, countdown, parcel
arrivals and crew routines run identically each time. It then draws one frame
per shot with the camera placed as recorded in
[`shots.json`](images/audit-2026-10/shots.json) (position, look-at target,
field of view, game time, and the subject's joint rotations or vehicle pose).
Shots framed by the game's own camera record the position it produced.

Repeatability was checked by running the script twice on the same build and
comparing the two `shots.json` files: every camera position, direction, vehicle
pose and joint rotation matched exactly for 19 of the 20 shots. The
exception is `crew-pose-squat`: John's patrol reaches his scripted squat at a
different moment each run (20.7 s and 23.3 s of shift time in the two runs),
so that shot is framed on him wherever he squats. The recorded clock stamps (`gameTime`,
`elapsedMs`) differ by a few milliseconds between runs because the game's own
clock runs for one real frame before the script takes it over; nothing visible
depends on them.

## 4. Why the movement is stiff

Every cause below is in the code; the screenshots confirm the result.

1. **Eleven pivots drive an eighty-bone rig.** `attachModel` maps only the
   head, upper arms, forearms, hands, thighs and shins
   (`characterModels.ts:233–243`). Rocketbox's spine chain, neck, clavicles,
   feet, toes and all finger bones keep their rest rotation forever. That is
   why the torso is a rigid block, the shoulders never rise with the arms, the
   feet never roll and the hands stay splayed in every shot.
2. **The walk is one sine wave.** `animateCharacter` sets hips to
   `±sin(phase)·0.5`, knees to a clipped cosine and arms to `∓sin(phase)·0.24`
   (`characters.ts:584–622`). There is no stance/swing distinction, no
   heel-strike, no weight shift beyond a 0.022 rad sway, and the phase advances
   by `dt·9·speed`, which is not tied to actual ground distance, so the feet
   slide whenever the speed and stride do not match.
3. **One exponential easing for everything.** Every joint target passes through
   `ease(current, target) = current + (target − current)·min(1, dt·14)`
   (`characters.ts:587`). That damps contacts (a foot never plants, it fades
   in) and makes every change of state feel the same: start walking, stop,
   pick up and put down all decay with the same 70 ms constant.
4. **Poses snap.** `Actor.animate` and `StageActor.animate` run the walk cycle,
   then zero the body and head and call `applyPose`, which writes joint
   rotations directly (`crew.ts:69–81`, `story.ts:76–91`). Entering a pose
   therefore happens in one frame; leaving it eases only the joints the walk
   cycle manages. Only `squat` has its own timed curve.
5. **Head look is workers-only.** `lookToward` runs for Termite and Skanky in
   `syncWorkers` (`main3d.ts:1250–1256`); crew and stage actors get
   `head.rotation.set(0, 0, 0)` every frame, so nobody else ever looks at
   anything. Where the look does run, it is pure neck yaw up to 1.0 rad
   with no chest or eye contribution (`crew-close-face`).
6. **The bone copy is exact and per frame.** `driveModel` writes
   `bone.quaternion = parent⁻¹ · pivot · rest` with no filtering
   (`characterModels.ts:329–335`), so the model can never move more
   naturally than the procedural figure. It also pulls the figure's hand pivots
   onto the model wrists so props follow, which is why a held box tracks
   correctly but the fingers around it do not.
7. **No root motion, IK or secondary motion.** Position comes from the game
   model; feet are placed by the hips, not the ground. A carried box is a fixed
   elbow angle (`−1.25 + 0.2·load`). Seated passengers are the standing figure
   with hips −0.41 m and thighs at −1.5 rad (`crew.ts:190–197`), posed onto a
   seat offset of 0.06 m (`story.ts:109`), not onto seat geometry.

**Does the joint-copy approach limit what is possible?** Yes, in two ways.
Everything the model does must first be expressible on the figure's eleven
pivots, so spine, clavicle, foot and finger motion are impossible without
extending the figure first. And because the copy replaces the bone rotations
every frame, authored clips cannot be layered under or blended with it; the
Rocketbox skeleton could play Mixamo-style clips through an `AnimationMixer`
(the devil already does) but only if the figure stops overwriting the bones for
those joints. The rig itself is not the limit: Rocketbox ships a full Biped
skeleton and the converter keeps it.

## 5. Why the vehicles look poor

1. **Polygon budget.** The sedans are 2,954 and 3,124 triangles including
   wheels, with hard-edged faceting across every panel
   (`sedan-exterior-front-left`). Kenney's `delivery.glb` is 2,476. These are
   game-jam assets; a mid-poly car with smooth normals needs roughly 15k–40k.
2. **Materials.** The Quaternius files have flat colour materials and no
   textures. `upgradeCar` forces `roughness 0.65, metalness 0.12` on every
   material (`propModels.ts:41`), so paint, chrome, rubber and plastic are the
   same dull surface. The Kenney colormap is a palette atlas, not a material.
3. **Glass.** The `Windows` material is made `transparent, opacity 0.3,
   depthWrite false` (`propModels.ts:43`). There is no transmission, no
   reflection, no tint gradient and no edge; glass reads as a grey film
   (`sedan-window-passengers`).
4. **Lighting and reflections.** The lot and road scenes have no environment
   map, so there is nothing for a car to reflect; the lot has a single
   directional light and point lights (`story.ts:344–356`), which flattens the
   paint. The depot's `RoomEnvironment` at intensity 0.18 is the only
   reflection source in the game, and no car is ever in the depot.
5. **No LOD.** Every model is drawn at its single detail level at any distance;
   the 28 traffic sedans and 127 buildings are all full-detail merges. This
   caps how much detail can be afforded on the player's own vehicle.
6. **Wheels and motion.** `mergeStatic(model)` at `propModels.ts:50` folds the
   four wheel meshes into the body, so they cannot rotate or steer;
   `DrivingView.update` sets position and yaw only (`driving.ts:266–293`).
7. **Occupant placement.** `StoryStage.seat` places a standing actor at
   `(−0.3, 0.06, ±0.36)` in the car frame and sets the `sit` pose
   (`story.ts:1650–1657`); there is no seat, so the figure's hips float at the
   right height while hands and feet pass through the body shell, and the only
   check is that the head stays below the 1.52 m roof line
   (`tests/story-clearance-browser.mjs:74`). The five-ton has no seated driver
   at all; the cab is solid.
8. **Scale.** The sedans are scaled non-uniformly to a 4.2 × 1.5 × 1.8 m
   box (`propModels.ts:27`), which stretches whatever proportions the artist
   chose; the wide variant is 6 % longer in the source yet squeezed to the
   same box.

## 7. The art-direction clash

The crew are photoreal: scanned-texture adults with individual faces, plaid
and denim, in a depot with photographed concrete, rubber and cardboard and a
screen-space ambient-occlusion pass. The vehicles and the city are the
opposite school: Quaternius and Kenney low-poly assets with flat palette
colours and no textures, designed for a stylised toy-box look. The two meet in
every arrival scene and on every drive. In `sedan-exterior-front-right` a
realistic Skanky sits inside a faceted cartoon sedan; in `city-street` the
photoreal driver is implied inside a toy truck on a toy street. The snow, the
GTAO and the ACES grade are tuned for the realistic side, which makes the
cartoon side look unfinished rather than deliberate.

**Recommendation: standardise on the realistic side ("grounded realism"),
not the stylised side.** Reasons:

- The investment and the tests are on the human side: 23 rigged, converted,
  licensed models with a converter, pose envelopes and clearance suites. Replacing
  them with stylised figures would discard that and re-baseline every pose test.
- The depot, the house, the props and the texture sets already follow the
  realistic direction and look the best of anything in the game.
- The stylised side is the smaller surface: two sedan files, one truck, five
  building types, two road pieces. Raising those to textured mid-poly models
  with real glass, interiors, separate wheels and an environment map is a
  bounded asset problem, and the fallbacks and `modelReady` plumbing already
  exist.
- The city can stay simpler than the depot as long as it reads as a real
  night street (textured facades, lit windows, wet asphalt, shadows), because
  the crew are never seen on foot in it.

The alternative, stylising the humans to match the cars, is cheaper per asset
but changes the identity of the game and throws away the Rocketbox work.

## 8. Decisions needed before a strategy can be written

1. **Animation approach.** (a) Keep the procedural figure and extend it (spine,
   clavicles, feet, fingers, better walk and transitions); (b) move to authored
   clips on the Rocketbox skeleton through `AnimationMixer` with a state
   machine, keeping the procedural path for software renderers; or (c) a
   hybrid where clips drive locomotion and the procedural layer only adds
   look-at and carried props. This decides whether the "no animation clips"
   rule and the joint-copy tests stay.
2. **Clip source and licence.** If clips are wanted: Mixamo (Adobe terms),
   CMU/other CC0 motion capture, or hand-authored. Rocketbox is MIT but ships
   no clips.
3. **Vehicle asset policy.** CC0 only (today's rule), CC BY with attribution
   in the game, or a paid licence; and a per-vehicle budget (triangles,
   texture size, file size). `docs/prop-models.md` already lists candidate
   truck sources that were not acquired.
4. **Interiors and an interior camera.** Whether the sedans and the five-ton
   get seats, dashboard and a driver, and whether a cab camera is added to the
   drive (which the chase-camera test and the driving-model test would need).
5. **City look.** Whether the driving city must match the depot's realism or
   may stay a lighter stylised set with better lighting, and whether the
   Kenney kit is kept, retextured or replaced.
6. **Performance targets.** The device class and frame budget the realism pass
   must hold (the current depot is about 1,180 draw calls and 460k triangles
   at the briefing per `docs/depot-quality-review.md`); whether LOD is
   required; and whether the software-renderer path must stay visually
   equivalent or may simply stay functional.
7. **Which tests may be re-baselined.** Specifically the vehicle box in
   `prop-model-browser.mjs`, the manifest hashes in `driving-assets.mjs`, the
   pose envelopes in `arm-motion-model.mjs` and `character-animation-model.mjs`,
   the seated roof bound in `story-clearance-browser.mjs`, and the pixel
   readback in `crew-work-browser.mjs`.
8. **Texture detail for the crew.** Whether to re-run the Rocketbox converter
   keeping normal and specular maps and 1024 px textures for everyone (file
   size roughly doubles against the 1.5 MB cap), which is the cheapest step
   toward less waxy faces.
